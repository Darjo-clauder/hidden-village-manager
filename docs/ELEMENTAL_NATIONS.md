# Elemental nations — making the five feel like five

Scope for giving each element a real identity: talent, skills, strengths and
weaknesses, missions, terrain, doctrine and colour. Written at `9abff53`.
Not started.

Decisions taken with Tyler before scoping:

- **Both sides get depth, the player's deeper.** Your nation is a build you
  live with for a run; rivals get enough to scout, recognise and prepare for.
- **Mostly real tradeoffs, with one genuine wall each.** Not a uniform ±12
  everywhere — each element has one spec it is simply bad at.
- **Missions in both directions** — signature contracts your element unlocks,
  and contracts generated by your dealings with other nations.
- **All four extras in scope**: nation-exclusive techniques, elemental terrain
  and weather, a nation doctrine track, element counter-matchups.

## 1. What already exists — do not rebuild these

| Thing | Where | State |
|---|---|---|
| Per-nation accent colour | `nations.js` + `_applyNationTheme` in `ui.js` | **Works, player only.** Retints `--accent` and derived tints. |
| Element affinity in talent | `nationTalent()` | ~60% element bias + school titles |
| Rival village identities | `VILLAGE_IDENTITIES` | 12 villages: style, statBias, element, archetypes |
| Minor nations | `MINOR_NATIONS` | 8, each with element + specialty stat |
| Clans / bloodlines | `clans.js` | 17, with passives |
| Jutsu + eligibility | `constants.js`, `shared/jutsu/eligibility.js` | 20 + 10 signatures; supports `altReq` |
| Combined elements | `combinedElements.js` | 10 pairs, counters, signature techniques |
| Village doctrines | `VILLAGE_DOCTRINES` | **3 generic** (fortress/commerce/sage) — see the collision note in §6 |
| Mission specs | mission templates | `combat, escort, intel, recovery, siege, stealth` (+ `null`) |

**Nation strengths today are `successMod` 0–4% and `ryoMod` −2–5%, with no
weaknesses at all.** That is the whole of it. Everything in §3 onward is new.

## 2. The architectural call: build on ELEMENT

There are currently three overlapping "nation" concepts:

1. `NATIONS` — five elemental nations (ember/tempest/tide/dune/stone), what the
   **player** picks; carries accent + traitMods.
2. `VILLAGE_IDENTITIES` — twelve great **rival villages**, each with an element.
3. `MINOR_NATIONS` — eight, each with an element.

They already agree on one thing: **element**. `NATIONS` maps 1:1 onto the five
(ember=Fire, tempest=Lightning, tide=Water, dune=Wind, stone=Earth).

So the identity package hangs off the **element**, and all three concepts read
from it. One table, three consumers. The player's nation resolves to an element;
a rival village already has one; a minor nation already has one. No new taxonomy,
and rivals inherit every strength and weakness the player gets — which is what
makes scouting them meaningful rather than decorative.

`shared/constants/elementalIdentity.js` becomes the single source of truth.
`NATIONS` keeps the accent/crest and gains a pointer to its element.

## 3. Strengths and weaknesses — the tuning table

Most cells are tradeoffs. Each element gets exactly **one +20 signature** and
**one −20 wall**. You can still take any mission; you feel it when you take the
wrong one.

| | combat | escort | intel | recovery | siege | stealth |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Fire** (Ember) | +10 | 0 | 0 | −10 | **+20** | **−20** |
| **Water** (Tide) | −10 | +10 | 0 | **+20** | **−20** | 0 |
| **Wind** (Dune) | −10 | 0 | +10 | 0 | **−20** | **+20** |
| **Earth** (Stone) | 0 | **+20** | −10 | 0 | +10 | **−20** |
| **Lightning** (Tempest) | **+20** | −10 | +10 | **−20** | 0 | 0 |

Values are percentage points on mission success, applied where
`_nationSuccessMod()` already plugs in — so the wiring exists.

Two deliberate properties:

- **Every spec except `intel` has exactly one owner.** With five elements and
  six specs one spec cannot have a +20, and intel is the right one to leave
  unowned: it belongs to the scouting and staff layers, not to chakra nature.
- **Siege and stealth are the sharpest axis** — two elements are excellent and
  two are walled on each. That is intentional: those are the two specs where
  "we are simply not built for this" reads as character rather than as a
  malfunction.

## 4. Workstreams

Eight, deliberately ordered so each ships alone.

### W1 — The identity table + strengths/weaknesses
`shared/constants/elementalIdentity.js`: the §3 matrix, plus per-element
talent bias, colour, crest, and the copy that describes each. Wire the spec
modifier into the existing `_nationSuccessMod()` seam for the player and the
equivalent rival path. **Ships alone and is felt immediately.**

### W2 — Rival colour identity
Extend `_applyNationTheme`'s accent system so rivals carry their element's
colour in the UI — league table, match build-up, diplomacy cards, the battle
viewer. Purely presentational; no sim change. Watch `_hexAlpha`, which has
already caused a silent `rgba(NaN,…)` failure once when handed a non-hex.

### W3 — Talent pools
Deepen `nationTalent()`: element bias moves from a flat ~60% to per-element
(a Fire nation should be *more* uniformly Fire than a Wind one), plus
element-flavoured stat leanings and name/title pools so a Tide dossier reads
differently from an Ember one at a glance.

### W4 — Nation-exclusive techniques
A short tree per element, gated by **nation** rather than clan — the gap that
made this ask: jutsu currently ignore nation entirely. Reuses `ALL_JUTSU` and
the `altReq` eligibility machinery shipped in `3d7e467`. Must obey the rule
that fell out of the rare-jutsu finding: **a technique nobody can reach is
content we did not write.** Every node needs a measured reachability check in
`depthCoverage`.

### W5 — Signature missions
A pool per element appended to the board when your nation matches. Mission
templates already carry `spec`, `rk`, `risk`, `mp` — this is data plus a filter
in `refreshMissionBoard`.

### W6 — Diplomatic missions
Contracts generated by standing with a specific village: sabotage when
relations sour, escort and joint operations when they warm. Hooks into the
existing `v.rel`, the alliance pacts and the vendetta ledger. This is where the
relations layer finally produces something you *do* rather than something you
read.

### W7 — Terrain and weather
Missions gain a terrain/weather tag that argues with element — Fire struggles
in a downpour, Wind suffers underground, Earth is at home in tunnels. Displayed
on the mission card so the player chooses knowingly. Pairs naturally with W5.

### W8 — Doctrine track
See the collision note in §6. Five or six element-specific nodes bought with
treasury — which doubles as another sink for the late-game money the economy
pass flagged as ballooning.

## 5. Element counter-matchups — and the trap

Tyler picked this, and it is the one item that can undo recent work if built
carelessly.

**The trap.** `MATCHDAY_AS_A_BET.md` exists because matchday had exactly one
correct answer per fixture, which made it a lookup table rather than a
decision. A base-element counter grid is the same shape. If the player can
*choose* their element per encounter, we have rebuilt the thing we just
removed.

**The rule that avoids it: a counter you cannot choose is texture; a counter
you pick each time is a lookup.** So:

- Counters apply where the player does **not** select the matchup — the league
  fixture list, mission antagonists, defence raids. There it is information to
  read and prepare around, not a dial.
- Magnitude stays at or below the ±5% the combined-element matchup already
  uses, so it colours a result without deciding one.
- It never becomes a per-fixture pick. The tactic layer owns that decision.

## 6. Watch-outs

- **`VILLAGE_DOCTRINES` already exists** — three generic doctrines
  (fortress/commerce/sage) with `defBonus`/`incomeMod`/`growthMod`, already
  consumed in the tick. W8 must **extend** that structure, not sit beside it,
  or the player has two unrelated things called doctrine.
- **`_hexAlpha` is fragile.** It has already produced `rgba(NaN,…)` across the
  entire UI once, from being handed `"var(--gold)"` instead of a hex. W2 feeds
  it many more values; validate at the boundary.
- **The player's element must be stable.** `G.vElement` is rolled at founding.
  Any of this that reads element needs to handle an old save that predates it,
  and the nation → element mapping needs a migration if `NATIONS` ids ever move.
- **Snapshots will move** on W1, W3, W4, W5, W7. Not on W2 (presentational) or
  W6 if it only adds gated content. Regenerate deliberately, and keep the
  24-seed invariant sweep green untouched.
- **Rival strengths must be scoutable, not free.** The intel gating shipped in
  `f4b6f0b` already hides a rival's *style* behind fresh intel. Their elemental
  strengths belong behind the same gate, or scouting quietly loses value.

## 7. Tests

- `elementalIdentity.test.js` — the matrix is complete and well formed; every
  element has exactly one +20 and one −20; no spec is universally punished; the
  values stay inside a declared band.
- **Balance:** no element may be strictly best. Assert that across the six
  specs each element wins at least one and loses at least one, and that summed
  modifiers across all specs are within a small tolerance of each other.
- `depthCoverage` — every nation-exclusive technique is actually learned, every
  signature mission actually appears, every terrain tag actually fires. This is
  the suite that caught three separate pieces of dead content already.
- Rival-vs-rival season sim: confirm the elemental spread does not reintroduce
  a standings bias like the fortress one (currently 0.73 places).

## 8. Sequencing

**W1 → W2 → W3** first: the table, then rivals wearing it, then talent
reflecting it. That trio alone delivers "each nation feels special" and is
worth shipping before anything else starts.

**W4 and W5** next — the two that make your own run distinct.

**W6, W7, W8** last; each is independent and none blocks the others.

Element counters (§5) land whenever, but **after** W1, since they read the same
table.

## 9. Open numbers

The §3 matrix, the ±5% counter cap, per-element talent bias strength, terrain
magnitudes, and the doctrine node costs. All tunable; none load-bearing on the
architecture. Expect one balance pass on the seeded harness after W1 and
another after W7.
